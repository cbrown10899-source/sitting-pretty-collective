How to take the photo — read this first ▾

The light you shoot in matters more than anything else. Five minutes of setup gives Chroma a clean read; a filtered or badly-lit photo bakes in a color cast it can't see past.

  1. Indirect daylight. Face a window or step into open shade — overcast is ideal. No direct sun, no golden hour.
  2. Lights off. Turn off lamps and bulbs; don't mix them with the window.
  3. White under your chin. A sheet of paper, a towel, or a white shirt over your shoulders — it blocks color from your clothes and gives the camera a true white.
  4. Bare face, hair back. No makeup, hair off the face.
  5. No filters or beauty mode. Turn off HDR and auto-enhance too.
  6. Check the white. If the paper looks truly white, you're good. Yellow or blue? Move and retake.
The white paper is your reference. It's the one thing whose true color you already know — if it reads clean white on screen, so will the rest of the shot.
